# Analyzing IMDb as an Organizing System
___
## 1. Primary Resources Being Organized

The two big resources are titles and people. 

A title page is usually a movie, a TV show, or one specific episode. It has the poster, year, runtime, star rating, genres, a short plot, and the cast. Pages like *Stranger Things* or a random episode work the same way. 

A person page is an actor, director, or writer. That page is mostly a list of what they have worked on in their careers or their "known for" titles. 

It also includes extra details, like reviews, trivia, or photos. New movies are another thing that keeps getting added. 

___

## 2. Primary Interactions the System Supports

Most people are not doing intense research on IMDb. They are trying to answer a quick question while either watching a show or talking to someone. 

The main interaction is search. You can type a name or title and check the year, cast, or runtime. That is basic fact-checking. 

Browsing is the other big one. If I do not have a title in mind, I can always look at Top 250, Popular TV, or a genre page and click around. 

I think this site is aimed more at regular viewers and not film students, for example. The whole point is to get an answer fast. 

## 3. Classification System

IMDb uses a **faceted** classification system. You can filter by:
- type of title (movie, series, episode, game)
- genre
- year
- country or language
- rating

One title can have more than one genre. *Obsession* is both horror and mystery. That works pretty well for normal users because nobody searches the same way. Some people like to start with an actor, and some just want comedies from 2005. 

___
## 4. How Relationships Are Represented

The relationships are what make IMDb useful. 

Click on a movie, and you get links to the cast and the crew. If you click on an actor, you can get every title they are credited on. TV shows nest episodes under the series page. There is also a "More Like This" section that suggests similar titles to what you are looking at.

All of those links are organizing systems. 

___
## 5. Closing

IMDb is less about storing perfect records and more about connecting titles and people. Search, browse, and "more like this" are more the interactions most people are actually using. The genre and title type filters are simple, but they work for a general audience. The weak spot is inconsistent tagging or smaller or harder-to-read titles.
